Select a day, then look under **Astronomical Events** in the inspector's **Day** tab. Chronologia lists events whose calculated instant falls on that day.

Astronomical events are displayed automatically; event-pack settings do not affect them.

## Seasons and Moon phases

Chronologia can show:

- March and September equinoxes
- June and December solstices
- New Moon
- First Quarter Moon
- Full Moon
- Last Quarter Moon

The time beside these events is labeled **TDB**: Barycentric Dynamical Time. It is an astronomical time scale, not your local civil time.

## Solar eclipses

Solar eclipses are identified as total, annular, hybrid, or partial. The inspector shows:

- The time of greatest eclipse, labeled **TD**
- Eclipse magnitude to four decimal places
- The Saros series number
- The source acknowledgement

**TD** is dynamical time rather than local clock time. Solar-eclipse predictions are credited in the app to Fred Espenak, NASA/GSFC.

### Show the eclipse path

For a total, annular, or hybrid eclipse, select the map button at the right side of the event. You can also double-click the event row.

The map displays:

- The central line in red
- The northern and southern limits of the central eclipse path in blue
- The location of greatest eclipse with a purple marker
- Compass, map scale, and zoom controls

The path is the central path of totality or annularity. Partial solar eclipses do not have such a path, so Chronologia does not offer the map button for them.

## Lunar eclipses

Lunar eclipses are identified as total, partial, or penumbral. The inspector shows:

- The time of greatest eclipse, labeled **TD**
- Penumbral magnitude
- Umbral magnitude
- The Saros series number
- The source acknowledgement

Lunar-eclipse predictions are credited in the app to Fred Espenak and Jean Meeus, NASA/GSFC. Chronologia does not currently provide a map for lunar eclipses.
